\section{What problem is ShapeMix-ATAC solving?}

Spatial ATAC-seq measures open chromatin in a tissue section.  Its fragments file records deduplicated DNA fragments assigned to spatial barcodes.  Each fragment provides two Tn5 cut sites, and a spot may contain more than one nucleus.  Therefore, the observed signal is a mixture.

\begin{bio}
Think of a spot as a small square drawn on top of tissue.  Inside that square, there may be T cells, B cells, fibroblasts, tumor cells, neurons, or other cell types.  ATAC-seq does not directly say which nucleus produced each fragment or cut site.  ShapeMix-ATAC tries to infer the hidden cell mixture from the cut-site counts and their parent fragments' lengths.
\end{bio}

The ordinary deconvolution question is:
\[
\text{observed mixed signal} \quad \Longrightarrow \quad \text{cell-type amounts in the spot}.
\]

The ShapeMix-ATAC question is richer:
\[
\begin{gathered}
\text{observed peak totals and}\\
\text{parent-fragment length composition}
\end{gathered}
\quad \Longrightarrow \quad
\begin{gathered}
\text{effective abundances and}\\
\text{normalized cell-type proportions}
\end{gathered}.
\]

\subsection{Why ATAC needs a special model}

Spatial transcriptomics mostly counts RNA molecules from genes.  Standard Cell Ranger ATAC peak matrices count Tn5 cut sites in open-chromatin peaks.  The parent fragments also have physical features:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Fragment length:} short fragments often come from nucleosome-free DNA; longer fragments can span nucleosomes.
    \item \textbf{Position inside a peak:} fragments may concentrate near the center, left edge, right edge, or flanking regions.
    \item \textbf{Motif-centered pattern:} cuts around a transcription-factor motif can carry footprint-like information.
\end{itemize}

\begin{idea}
The primary ShapeMix data object is a three-dimensional count table:
\[
\boxed{Y_{s,p,b} = \text{deduplicated Tn5 cut sites in spot }s,\text{ peak }p,\text{ parent-length bin }b.}
\]
Each cut site inherits the length bin of its parent fragment.  A usual Cell Ranger-compatible peak-count model uses only \(N_{s,p}=\sum_b Y_{s,p,b}\).  ShapeMix keeps the extra index \(b\).  A unique-fragment-count analysis would be a separately versioned sensitivity analysis with its own matched baseline.
\end{idea}

The primary implementation uses exactly three parent-fragment length bins:
\[
 b=1: [0,100), \qquad b=2: [100,250), \qquad b=3: [250,\infty)\ \text{bp}.
\]
Length is \(\texttt{chromEnd}-\texttt{chromStart}\) from the adjusted, zero-based half-open 10x fragment interval. The right-cut coordinate convention must be validated by reconstructing selected columns of the official peak matrix before canonical data are frozen.

\section{A short Bayesian warm-up}

Bayesian modeling means we write down a story for how the data could have been generated.  Then we run the story backward.

\begin{mathbox}{Bayes rule in words}
\[
\text{posterior} \; \propto \; \text{likelihood} \times \text{prior}.
\]
In ShapeMix-ATAC:
\[
\begin{aligned}
p(&\text{hidden cell mixture} \given \text{observed ATAC cut-site tensor})\\
&\propto
p(\text{observed ATAC cut-site tensor} \given \text{hidden cell mixture})\\
&\qquad{}\times p(\text{hidden cell mixture}).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{mathbox}

\section{Start with the ordinary peak-count model}

The primary model fixes reference signatures and infers only spot abundances.  Let
\(R_{i,p,b}\) be the training-reference cut-site count for cell \(i\), peak \(p\),
and parent-length bin \(b\), and define \(T_{i,p}=\sum_bR_{i,p,b}\).  From these
training cells we estimate \(A_{c,p}\), the mean total cut-site rate per reference
cell of type \(c\), \(\omega_{c,p,b}\), its conditional parent-length-bin
distribution, and one global per-reference-cell inverse-dispersion
\(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\).

Let \(z_{s,c}>0\) be the unknown effective abundance and
\(e_s=\sum_cz_{s,c}\).  The ordinary peak total and its expected value are
\begin{align}
N_{s,p} &= \sum_bY_{s,p,b},\\
\mu_{s,p} &= \sum_c z_{s,c}A_{c,p}. \label{eq:mu}
\end{align}
The count-only model is
\begin{mathbox}{Shared total-count model}
\begin{align}
N_{s,p} &\sim \NB\!\left(\text{mean}=\mu_{s,p},
\text{dispersion}=\max(e_s,\epsilon)\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right). \label{eq:totalnb}
\end{align}
\end{mathbox}
There is no observed spot-depth offset in the primary model.  Total captured depth
is absorbed into \(z\).

\section{The ShapeMix-ATAC likelihood}

\subsection{The factorized core equation}

ShapeMix preserves Equation~\eqref{eq:totalnb} and adds a conditional allocation of
the same total among parent-length bins.  Define
\begin{align}
v_{s,p,b} &= \sum_c z_{s,c}A_{c,p}\omega_{c,p,b}, \label{eq:v}\\
\mu_{s,p} &= \sum_bv_{s,p,b}=\sum_cz_{s,c}A_{c,p},\\
\rho_{s,p,b} &= \frac{v_{s,p,b}}{\mu_{s,p}}. \label{eq:rho}
\end{align}
The primary same-dataset simulation has zero background.  Its complete likelihood is:

\begin{mathbox}{Core ShapeMix likelihood}
\begin{align}
N_{s,p} &\sim \NB\!\left(\text{mean}=\mu_{s,p},
\text{dispersion}=\max(e_s,\epsilon)\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right),\\
\boldsymbol{Y}_{s,p,\cdot}\mid N_{s,p}
&\sim \Mult\!\left(N_{s,p},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{s,p,\cdot}\right). \label{eq:shapeconditional}
\end{align}
\end{mathbox}

The first line asks how many cut sites occur in the peak.  The second asks how that
fixed total is distributed among the three parent-fragment length bins.  The new
ShapeMix information is \(\omega_{c,p,b}\), the reference conditional shape.

\begin{warningbox}
Independent negative-binomial likelihoods for \(Y_{s,p,b}\) are not the primary
model.  Their sum is generally not the same negative-binomial total as the
count-only arm.  They remain only as a clearly named future sensitivity model.
\end{warningbox}

\subsection{Negative-binomial parameterization}

This document uses a negative binomial with mean \(\mu\) and inverse-dispersion \(\phi\):
\[
X \sim \NB(\text{mean}=\mu,\text{dispersion}=\phi)
\]
means
\[
\E[X]=\mu,
\qquad
\Var(X)=\mu+\frac{\mu^2}{\phi}.
\]

A spot uses \(\phi=\max(e_s,\epsilon)\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\).  Scaling by effective
abundance approximates aggregation of independent reference-depth cells.  The
conditional log likelihood is evaluated stably as
\[
\log p(\boldsymbol{Y}\mid N,\boldsymbol{\rho})=
\log\Gamma(N+1)-\sum_b\log\Gamma(Y_b+1)
+\sum_{b:Y_b>0}Y_b\log\rho_b.
\]
Rows with \(N=0\) contribute exactly zero.  In the Poisson-total special case, this
factorization is algebraically equivalent to independent Poisson bin counts; that
identity is an implementation test.

\subsection{The exact count-only ablation}

The two objectives are
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{count}}(z)
&=\log p(N\mid z,A,\phi_{\mathrm{ref}})+\log p(z),\\
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{shape}}(z)
&=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{count}}(z)
+\log p(Y\mid N,z,A,\omega).
\end{align*}
Everything except the final conditional shape term is identical: outer split,
inner mixture, peaks, \(N\), \(A\), \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\), abundance prior,
parameters, initialization, optimizer, stopping rule, seed, and compute budget.
If all cell types have identical \(\omega_{c,p,\cdot}\), the shape term contains no
information about \(z\), so both arms should agree within numerical tolerance.

The model can be taught as a recipe:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Use only training-reference cells to select peaks and estimate \(A\), \(\omega\), and \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\).
    \item Form pseudo-spots only from held-out test cells.
    \item Infer positive \(z_{s,c}\) from the shared total-count likelihood.
    \item For ShapeMix only, add the conditional parent-length composition likelihood.
    \item Normalize \(z\) to obtain cell-type proportions and evaluate paired runs.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Reference signatures}

The primary model treats signatures as empirical inputs, not inferred latent
variables.  This avoids trying to decompose the same scale simultaneously among
abundance, cell-type yield, peak detectability, and accessibility.

Let \(\mathcal{I}_c\) be training-reference cells of type \(c\), \(g_p\) a pooled
peak-rate target, \(u_{p,b}\) the pooled training-reference parent-length
distribution for peak \(p\), and \(h_i=1\) for the primary same-protocol
experiment.  Define a dataset-global distribution and use the same frozen
\(\alpha_\omega=1\) to smooth the pooled peak distribution toward it:
\begin{align}
u_b^{\mathrm{global}}
&=\frac{\sum_{i,p}R_{i,p,b}}{\sum_{i,p,b'}R_{i,p,b'}},\\
u_{p,b}
&=\frac{\sum_i R_{i,p,b}+\alpha_\omega u_b^{\mathrm{global}}}
{\sum_{i,b'}R_{i,p,b'}+\alpha_\omega}.
\end{align}
Every global bin must have positive training-reference support.  Cell-type
signatures then use
\begin{align}
A_{c,p}
&=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_c}T_{i,p}+\alpha_Ag_p}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_c}h_i+\alpha_A}, \label{eq:A}\\
\omega_{c,p,b}
&=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_c}R_{i,p,b}+\alpha_\omega u_{p,b}}
{\sum_{i\in\mathcal{I}_c}T_{i,p}+\alpha_\omega}. \label{eq:omega}
\end{align}
Sparse peaks shrink first toward their pooled peak distribution and then toward the
dataset-global distribution.  The concentration is frozen by the benchmark
protocol and is not tuned on held-out mixtures.

\begin{bio}
If T-cell cut sites at peak \(p\) usually have short parent fragments, then
\(\omega_{T,p,\mathrm{short}}\) is high.  If B-cell cut sites at the same peak
mostly have mononucleosomal-length parents, ShapeMix can use that conditional
difference even when \(A_{T,p}\) and \(A_{B,p}\) are similar.
\end{bio}

\subsection{Cross-fitted global dispersion}

Inside the training-reference pool, make a deterministic, cell-type-stratified
two-fold split.  For each cell \(i\), estimate its held-out mean \(m_{i,p}\) from
the opposite fold's cell-type signature.  Then compute
\begin{align}
\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{raw}}
&=\frac{\sum_{i,p}\left[(T_{i,p}-m_{i,p})^2-m_{i,p}\right]}
{\sum_{i,p}m_{i,p}^2},\\
\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}&=\max\!\left(\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}^{\mathrm{raw}},10^{-8}\right),\\
\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}&=1/\alpha_{\mathrm{ref}}. \label{eq:dispersion}
\end{align}
The fold seed, numerator, denominator, and resulting values are recorded.  A
non-finite or zero denominator is an error.  After cross-fitting, \(A\) is
re-estimated from all training-reference cells.  Peak-specific or learned
dispersion is a future sensitivity analysis.

\section{Prior and semantics for hidden abundance}

The only inferred biological quantity in the primary MAP model is \(z_{s,c}>0\).
A simple fixed Gamma prior is
\begin{align}
z_{s,c}&\sim\Gam\left(r_z,\frac{r_z}{m_c}\right),
&\E[z_{s,c}]&=m_c. \label{eq:zprior}
\end{align}
The implementation uses \(z=\operatorname{softplus}(z^{\mathrm{raw}})+\epsilon\)
and adds the corresponding log prior to the objective.

\begin{warningbox}
The primary model has no observed library-size offset \(\ell_s\).  Because total
captured depth is absorbed into \(z\), \(z_{s,c}\) is an effective
reference-cell-equivalent, depth-scaled abundance.  It is not guaranteed to be an
integer or a calibrated nucleus count.  The standardized scientific output is
\[
\pi_{s,c}=\frac{z_{s,c}}{\sum_{c'}z_{s,c'}}.
\]
\end{warningbox}

\section{Complete core model}

Putting the primary pieces together:

\begin{mathbox}{Complete core ShapeMix-ATAC model}
\begin{align}
v_{s,p,b}&=\sum_cz_{s,c}A_{c,p}\omega_{c,p,b},\\
\mu_{s,p}&=\sum_bv_{s,p,b},\qquad
\rho_{s,p,b}=v_{s,p,b}/\mu_{s,p},\\
N_{s,p}&\sim\NB\!\left(\mu_{s,p},\max(e_s,\epsilon)\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\right),\\
\boldsymbol{Y}_{s,p,\cdot}\mid N_{s,p}
&\sim\Mult\!\left(N_{s,p},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{s,p,\cdot}\right),\\
z_{s,c}&\sim\Gam\left(r_z,r_z/m_c\right),\qquad e_s=\sum_cz_{s,c}.
\end{align}
\end{mathbox}

The fixed quantities \(A\), \(\omega\), and \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\) come only from
training-reference cells.  The output cell-type proportion is computed from MAP
abundances:
\[
\pi_{s,c}=\frac{z_{s,c}}{\sum_{c'=1}^{C} z_{s,c'}}.
\]

\section{Richer Bayesian components are future extensions}

The earlier model draft included a learned background \(\beta_{p,b}\), cell-type
yield \(q_c\), peak detectability \(d_p\), latent accessibility
\(\theta_{c,p}\), inferred \(\omega\), spatial effects \(u_{s,c}\), peak-specific
dispersion \(\phi_p\), and variational uncertainty.  These remain useful research
directions, but none belongs to the primary ablation.  Estimating all of them with
\(z\) would create scale and attribution ambiguities and could let background or
spatial smoothing absorb the shape signal.

Extensions must be introduced one at a time after the three-bin MAP model passes
its recovery, conservation, and negative-control tests.  A fixed small background
is the first robustness check; a Dirichlet--multinomial conditional shape model is
the first shape-overdispersion check.  Learned background, cross-protocol scaling,
spatial smoothing, richer position or motif axes, learned signatures, and
variational inference follow only when residual diagnostics justify them.  The
independent-bin negative-binomial formulation is a sensitivity model, never the
count-only control for the primary scientific claim.

\section{Worked example: why shape helps}

Suppose there are only two cell types: T cells and B cells.  At one peak, both cell types have the same total accessibility, so a peak-count-only model cannot distinguish them.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{L{0.28\linewidth}C{0.2\linewidth}C{0.2\linewidth}C{0.2\linewidth}}
\toprule
Cell type / spot & Total cut sites at peak & Short-parent cut sites & Mono-parent cut sites \\
\midrule
T-cell reference & 100 & 80\% & 20\% \\
B-cell reference & 100 & 20\% & 80\% \\
Observed spot & 100 & 60\% & 40\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Peak-count model sees only:
\[
100 \approx 100.
\]
It learns almost nothing.

ShapeMix sees:
\[
0.60 \approx 0.80\pi_T+0.20(1-\pi_T).
\]
Solving:
\begin{align*}
0.60 &= 0.80\pi_T+0.20-0.20\pi_T,\\
0.40 &= 0.60\pi_T,\\
\pi_T &\approx 0.67.
\end{align*}

So the observed spot looks like roughly:

\]

\section{What the primary MAP estimate means}

For fixed \(A\), \(\omega\), and \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\), the primary model finds
\[
\widehat z=\arg\max_{z>0}
\left\{\log p(N\mid z,A,\phi_{\mathrm{ref}})
+\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{shape}}\log p(Y\mid N,z,A,\omega)
+\log p(z)\right\}.
\]
The reported estimate is
\(\widehat\pi_{s,c}=\widehat z_{s,c}/\sum_{c'}\widehat z_{s,c'}\).
It is a point estimate.  The MAP MVP does not produce credible intervals, and its
outputs must not be described as posterior means or calibrated uncertainty.

\begin{plainbox}{Future uncertainty model}
Uncertainty remains scientifically valuable, especially for rare cell types.
Variational inference may be added after the MAP likelihood, gradients, recovery,
and negative controls are validated.  That extension must be reported as a new
model version rather than attributed to the MVP.
\end{plainbox}

\section{Fitting the model}

\subsection{Primary: MAP estimation}

MAP means maximum a posteriori.  It finds the single parameter setting that maximizes:
\[
\log p(N\given z)+\mathbb{1}_{\mathrm{shape}}\log p(Y\given N,z)+\log p(z),
\]
with only \(z\) inferred.  Initialize from collapsed-count NNLS with a uniform
fallback, use deterministic restarts, optimize with Adam, and fail on non-finite
losses, gradients, abundances, or proportions.  Process peaks and spots in chunks
rather than materializing a dense \(S\times P\times B\) expected tensor.

\subsection{Future: variational inference}

Variational inference approximates the posterior with a simpler distribution:
\[
q(z) \approx p(z\given Y),
\]

Then it optimizes an evidence lower bound, usually called the ELBO:
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ELBO}}
= \E_q[\log p(Y,\Theta)] - \E_q[\log q(\Theta)].
\]

This can provide approximate uncertainty, but it is not part of the primary
experiment and should not be implemented until MAP behavior is credible.

\section{Leakage-free experimental structure}

The available PBMC input contains one donor.  Therefore the primary experiment
uses deterministic, cell-type-stratified 70/30 cell splits rather than claiming a
donor split.  Each \emph{outer split seed} defines one reference/test partition.
Peak selection, smoothing, signatures, and dispersion use only the 70\% reference
pool.  Each \emph{inner mixture seed} samples pseudo-spots only from that outer
split's held-out 30\% pool.

Both arms run on the exact same pseudo-spots for every
\((\text{outer seed},\text{inner seed},\text{condition})\) tuple.  Inner-mixture
effects are first averaged within each outer split before describing variability
across outer splits.  Neither spots nor inner mixtures are independent biological
replicates.  Intervals from this design quantify conditional resampling variability
within one donor and do not establish donor-level generalization.

\section{How to build the shape-count tensor}

\subsection{Step-by-step preprocessing}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Start with the BGZF/tabix-indexed five-column 10x fragments file: chromosome, start, end, barcode, and \texttt{readSupport}.
    \item Treat each row as one deduplicated fragment and ignore \texttt{readSupport} in the primary analysis.
    \item Compute parent length \(L=\texttt{chromEnd}-\texttt{chromStart}\) and assign both of its Tn5 cut sites to one fixed length bin.
    \item Assign each cut site to the unique selected, non-overlapping Cell Ranger peak that contains it.  Validate the right-cut coordinate against the vendor peak matrix first.
    \item Select 5,000 peaks by a deterministic accessibility-variance rank computed from reference cells only; never use held-out cells or shape counts for selection.
    \item Accumulate sparse cell/spot-by-peak matrices for each bin and set \(N_{s,p}=\sum_bY_{s,p,b}\).
    \item Require the layer sum to equal the ordinary peak-count matrix exactly and record source, feature, and split hashes.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Fragment-length bin example}

For fragment length \(L\):
\[
 b(L)=
 \begin{cases}
 1, & L < 100 \quad \text{short},\\
 2, & 100 \le L < 250 \quad \text{mono-nucleosomal-like},\\
 3, & L \ge 250 \quad \text{long}.
 \end{cases}
\]

These half-open cutoffs are fixed for the primary experiment.  Alternative cutoffs
are versioned sensitivity analyses, not tuning on final mixtures.

\section{Diagnostics and ablation tests}

\subsection{Diagnostics}

After fitting, check:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{Conservation:} bin layers sum exactly to \(N\), and selected columns reconstruct or explain the official vendor matrix.
    \item \textbf{Count reconstruction:} compare fitted \(\mu\) with \(N\) by peak and spot.
    \item \textbf{Shape-bin reconstruction:} does the model reproduce short/mono/long ratios?
    \item \textbf{Optimization:} all losses, gradients, abundances, and proportions are finite; restarts agree or their disagreement is reported.
    \item \textbf{Homogenized shape:} identical \(\omega\) across cell types makes ShapeMix agree with count-only within tolerance.
    \item \textbf{Permuted shape:} permuting cell-type shape labels must not create a credible improvement.
    \item \textbf{Poisson identity:} factorized Poisson total plus conditional multinomial equals independent Poisson bin counts.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablation tests}

Ablation means removing one ingredient at a time to test whether it matters.

\section{What makes ShapeMix-ATAC new and testable?}

The standard transferred deconvolution methods use spot-by-feature count matrices.  For ATAC, the feature is usually a peak.  ShapeMix augments that matrix with a tensor whose entries use the same count unit:
\[
\text{ordinary ATAC deconvolution: } N_{s,p}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{ShapeMix-ATAC: }Y_{s,p,b}.
\]

\begin{idea}
The narrow scientific claim is: \emph{does the parent-fragment length composition
of observed insertions improve deconvolution beyond their peak totals?}
\end{idea}

This is especially useful for:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item closely related immune subtypes,
    \item nearby developmental states,
    \item tumor and stromal mixtures,
    \item rare cell types with subtle peak-count differences,
    \item cell types that share open peaks but differ in parent-fragment length patterns.
\end{itemize}

\section{Implementation pseudocode}

\begin{plainbox}{Pseudocode}
\begin{verbatim}
Input:
    BGZF fragments file plus tabix index
    non-overlapping Cell Ranger peaks
    scATAC/multiome reference with cell type labels

Preprocess:
    make deterministic stratified 70/30 outer cell split
    choose 5,000 peaks from reference cells only
    ignore readSupport; bin each deduplicated parent fragment
    assign its two cut sites to containing peaks
    build sparse layers Y[s,p,b] and totals N[s,p]
    estimate fixed A[c,p], omega[c,p,b], phi_ref from reference

For each nested mixture seed and each arm:
    form the same pseudo-spots from held-out cells
    initialize z[s,c] from collapsed-count NNLS
    repeat until convergence:
        compute mu[s,p] and abundance-scaled NB total loss
        if shape arm: add conditional multinomial loss
        update only positive z[s,c] plus its fixed prior

Output:
    pi[s,c] = z[s,c] / sum_c z[s,c]
    effective depth-scaled abundance z[s,c]
    convergence and restart diagnostics
    residual peaks and residual shape patterns
\end{verbatim}
\end{plainbox}

\section{Common questions}

\subsection{Is \texorpdfstring{\(\omega_{c,p,b}\)}{omega} inferred from each spot?}

No.  In the primary model, \(\omega\) is a fixed, smoothed empirical input
estimated only from training-reference cells.  Inferring it jointly with abundance
is a future model version.

\subsection{Why is there no \texorpdfstring{\(\theta_{c,p}\)}{theta}, yield, or detectability term?}

Their product is absorbed into the fixed total-rate signature \(A_{c,p}\).  This
keeps the MVP identifiable enough to test shape without asking the model to divide
one scale among \(z\), yield, detectability, and accessibility.

\subsection{Is \texorpdfstring{\(z_{s,c}\)}{z} a cell count?}

No.  It is an effective reference-cell-equivalent, depth-scaled abundance.  It may
track cell number in a same-protocol simulation, but without an observed depth
offset or external calibration it is neither an exact integer nor a nucleus count.
The primary biological output is the normalized proportion \(\pi\).

\subsection{Can we use Poisson instead of negative binomial?}

Yes.  Replace only the shared total model with
\(N_{s,p}\sim\Pois(\mu_{s,p})\) and retain the same conditional multinomial.
This is also a golden test because the resulting joint distribution equals
independent Poisson bin counts with means \(v_{s,p,b}\).

\subsection{Do we need all peaks?}

No.  The primary run uses 5,000 peaks selected by a frozen reference-only
accessibility-variance ranking.  Runs with 10,000 and 20,000 peaks are sensitivity
analyses.  Selecting peaks from held-out mixtures or from shape differences would
leak test information into the model.

\section{Fixed evaluation contract}

The dataset declares one ordered cell-type universe, which must equal the truth
columns and reference-label universe.  Prediction rows must contain the exact truth
spot set.  A missing declared prediction type is inserted as zero so omission is
penalized; an unknown extra type is an error.  Duplicate labels, non-numeric,
non-finite, negative, or all-zero rows fail evaluation.  Truth and prediction rows
must already sum to one within the frozen tolerance; they are not silently
renormalized and invalid rows are never dropped.

The primary metrics are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \textbf{RMSE:} root mean squared error across all
    \(S\times C\) entries in the fixed universe.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{jsd\_v2}:} mean per-spot base-2 Jensen--Shannon
    \emph{divergence},
    \[
    \frac{1}{S}\sum_s
    \operatorname{JSD}_{2}(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\mathrm{true}}_s,
    \widehat{\boldsymbol{\pi}}_s),
    \]
    operationally
    \texttt{mean(jensenshannon(truth, prediction, base=2)$^2$)}.  Its range is
    \([0,1]\).  The square is required because the library function returns
    Jensen--Shannon distance; the historical unsquared value is a different metric.
\end{itemize}

Secondary metrics include per-type MAE and RMSE, correlation, frozen rare-cell
precision, recall, F1, and AUPRC, plus runtime and memory.  The metric version and
declared universe are saved with every comparison.

\section{Optional extensions after the primary test}

Image-derived nuclei budgets, peak reliability weights, a fixed background,
cross-protocol scaling, a Dirichlet--multinomial shape term, spatial smoothing,
position or motif axes, Bayesian signatures, and variational uncertainty may all be
valuable.  Each changes the model or claim and therefore requires a versioned
dataset/configuration, its own negative control, and an identifiability check.  None
is part of the first ShapeMix-versus-count-only comparison.

\section{Recommended first experiment}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item Make frozen, stratified 70/30 outer splits and document the one-donor limitation.
    \item From each reference pool only, choose 5,000 peaks and estimate \(A\), \(\omega\), and cross-fitted \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\).
    \item Build matched pseudo-spots from held-out cells with mixture seeds nested inside each split seed.
    \item Fit the shared negative-binomial total model to both arms; add only the conditional three-bin term for ShapeMix.
    \item Pair results within every outer-seed, inner-seed, and condition tuple.  Average inner effects within outer splits before summarizing across splits.
    \item Report RMSE and \texttt{jsd\_v2}, plus frozen secondary metrics, runtime, convergence, and negative controls, even if shape does not help.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{warningbox}
Do not treat spots, inner mixtures, or repeated partitions of one donor as
independent biological replicates.  The primary result is evidence conditional on
this dataset; cross-donor validation requires additional donors.
\end{warningbox}

\section{Summary in one page}

\begin{mathbox}{The whole primary model in two lines}
\begin{align*}
N_{s,p}&\sim\NB\!\left(
\mu_{s,p}=\sum_cz_{s,c}A_{c,p},
\max\!\left(\sum_cz_{s,c},\epsilon\right)\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}
\right),\\
\boldsymbol{Y}_{s,p,\cdot}\mid N_{s,p}
&\sim\Mult\!\left(N_{s,p},
\frac{\sum_cz_{s,c}A_{c,p}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{c,p,\cdot}}
{\sum_cz_{s,c}A_{c,p}}\right).
\end{align*}
\end{mathbox}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
    \item \(Y_{s,p,b}\): deduplicated cut sites in a peak, grouped by their parent fragment's fixed length bin.
    \item \(N_{s,p}=\sum_bY_{s,p,b}\): the identical peak total used by both arms.
    \item \(A_{c,p}\): fixed reference total-rate signature.
    \item \(\omega_{c,p,b}\): fixed conditional parent-length signature.
    \item \(\phi_{\mathrm{ref}}\): fixed, cross-fitted global reference inverse-dispersion.
    \item \(z_{s,c}\): inferred effective reference-cell-equivalent, depth-scaled abundance.
\end{itemize}

The final cell-type composition is:
\[
\pi_{s,c}=\frac{z_{s,c}}{\sum_{c'}z_{s,c'}}.
\]

\begin{idea}
ShapeMix-ATAC asks one controlled question: \emph{does parent-fragment length
composition improve deconvolution beyond peak totals?}  The count-only arm uses the
exact same total likelihood and optimization; ShapeMix adds only the conditional
length-composition term.
\end{idea}
